Mono PERC, TOPCon, and panel selection

Panel technology matters, but layout, shade, electrical design, warranty support, and workmanship determine whether the complete system performs well.

By Your Energy Editorial Team7 min read

Solar quotations often reduce panel selection to a brand and wattage. That is not enough. A module is a specific model with physical dimensions, electrical ratings, cell technology, glass and frame construction, connector details, mechanical ratings, warranties, and installation requirements.

Quick answer

TOPCon and Mono PERC can both be used in strong projects. Compare exact datasheets, usable roof area, module dimensions, temperature coefficient, warranty terms, mechanical ratings, supply traceability, inverter compatibility, and local claim support.

What Mono PERC means

Mono PERC modules use monocrystalline silicon cells with a passivated rear-cell design intended to improve light use compared with older conventional cell structures. The technology is widely available and can offer a mature equipment and service ecosystem.

What TOPCon means

TOPCon refers to a tunnel oxide passivated contact cell architecture. Current products commonly position it as a higher-efficiency, lower-degradation, or stronger temperature-performance option, but the actual advantage must be checked on the exact manufacturer datasheet and commercial terms.

Wattage is not the same as quality

A 600 W module is not automatically better than a 550 W module. It may be physically larger, use a different cell layout, carry higher current, or simply reduce the number of modules required for the same nominal capacity. The design must verify that the chosen inverter, connectors, cable, structure, and roof layout support it.

What about bifacial modules?

Bifacial modules can collect light from the rear as well as the front. The benefit depends on rear-side exposure, roof reflectivity, height, row spacing, shading, and mounting design. A bifacial label does not guarantee a fixed extra yield on every roof.

Brands and approved model choices

A reputable manufacturer is useful, but the quotation should still name the exact model. Your Energy quotations may offer choices from UTL Solar, Adani, Waaree, Genus, Luminous, or an agreed combined selection, subject to design, approved requirements, supply availability, and customer preference. Final equipment should be documented before installation.

Seven checks before approval

  1. Ask for the current datasheet and warranty document.
  2. Verify the model and quantity shown in the bill of material.
  3. Confirm the layout uses actual module dimensions.
  4. Check inverter voltage and current compatibility.
  5. Confirm connectors and cable are properly matched.
  6. Understand who handles a warranty claim and site visit.
  7. Record serial numbers and installation photographs at handover.
Better buying rule

Select the complete engineered system, not the panel sticker. Excellent modules cannot correct persistent shade, weak structure, unsafe connections, or poor service.
