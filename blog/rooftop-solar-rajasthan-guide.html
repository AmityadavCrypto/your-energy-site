Rooftop solar in Rajasthan: a practical guide

How to plan around strong sunlight, high temperatures, dust, roof conditions, electricity usage, and the current DISCOM process.

By Your Energy Editorial Team2 August 20267 min read

Rajasthan has excellent solar potential, but a good project is not created by sunlight alone. The system must fit the customer's electricity pattern, roof, sanctioned load, local wind conditions, cleaning access, and applicable DISCOM process.

Quick answer

Begin with twelve months of electricity bills and a physical roof survey. Then confirm shading, structure, cable route, inverter location, sanctioned load, proposed capacity, metering route, and a safe maintenance plan before approving equipment or price.

Start with the bill, not a standard package

Collect a full year of bills because cooling loads can change sharply by season. Note units consumed, billing category, sanctioned load, demand charges where relevant, and any planned additions such as air-conditioning, pumps, machinery, or electric vehicles. The useful capacity is the one that aligns generation with realistic consumption and the applicable export arrangement.

Design for Rajasthan conditions

Heat

Solar modules produce power in sunlight, but module temperature also affects output. Good airflow below the modules, suitable cable selection, correctly rated electrical equipment, and a shaded, ventilated inverter location matter.

Dust

Dust accumulation can reduce performance. The design should provide safe access and a sensible cleaning route without requiring people to step on modules or stretch across unsafe roof edges. Cleaning frequency should follow actual soiling and monitored performance, not a fixed promise.

Wind and structure

Mounting structure design must consider roof type, fixing method, corrosion protection, local wind exposure, and waterproofing. A rooftop system should not be treated as furniture placed on a terrace.

Understand the approval route

Residential consumers seeking central assistance should follow the official PM Surya Ghar process. Other grid-connected projects should confirm the currently applicable Rajasthan regulations, DISCOM portal, technical feasibility, metering, and commissioning steps. These requirements can change, and different connection categories may follow different routes.

  1. Verify the consumer number, tariff category, sanctioned load, and name on the bill.
  2. Confirm which DISCOM serves the location and use the current official application channel.
  3. Obtain technical feasibility before treating the proposed system as approved.
  4. Complete installation according to approved capacity and technical requirements.
  5. Coordinate inspection, metering, commissioning, and final documentation.

Questions to ask before signing

  • What annual consumption and daytime load were used for sizing?
  • Has shade been checked across different hours and seasons?
  • What are the exact module and inverter models?
  • What structure material, section, coating, fasteners, and foundation method are included?
  • Are AC/DC protection, earthing, lightning protection where required, cables, conduits, labels, and monitoring included?
  • Who handles portal work, metering coordination, and document corrections?
  • What is excluded from the price?
  • Who provides local service after commissioning?

Plan maintenance at design stage

A monitoring app is useful only when someone reviews it. Establish a baseline after commissioning, save monthly generation records, investigate sustained drops, keep drains clear, visually inspect for loose or damaged components, and schedule qualified electrical checks. Safety comes before cleaning frequency.

Regulatory note

Rajasthan regulations and DISCOM procedures can be amended. Check the current RERC regulation list, the official consumer portal, and the instructions issued by your DISCOM before committing funds.
