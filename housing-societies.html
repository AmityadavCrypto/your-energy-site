Where Solar Helps

Target the electricity costs shared by the whole community.

Society solar is usually planned around common facilities. The right design starts by matching daytime generation with the loads and meters the association is responsible for.

01

Lifts and common lighting

Review operating hours, connected load, meter arrangement, and daytime consumption before sizing.

02

Water pumps and treatment

Map pumping schedules and high-demand equipment so solar generation is used effectively.

03

Security and facility systems

Include gates, CCTV, fire systems, offices, clubhouses, and other recurring common loads.

04

Parking and future EV loads

Plan cable routes, distribution capacity, and future demand without confusing solar generation with backup.

05

Multiple blocks or meters

Check how buildings, meters, sanctioned loads, and applicable DISCOM rules affect the project structure.

06

Shared roof and terrace use

Balance available area with water tanks, access paths, services, shading, and resident use of the terrace.

Funding Routes

Choose an ownership model the society can explain and sustain.

Availability and terms depend on project size, lender or investor approval, location, and current regulation. Every option should be compared on total cost, control, responsibility, and long-term savings.

01

Society-owned CAPEX

The society funds and owns the asset. This can offer strong long-term value, but requires approved capital and clear maintenance responsibility.

02

Loan or structured finance

Ownership remains with the society while payments are spread over time, subject to lender eligibility, security, pricing, and documentation.

03

Third-party OPEX / RESCO

An external party may invest and sell solar power under a contract. Review tariff, tenure, guarantees, roof rights, exit terms, and maintenance carefully.

Safety And Quality

A society roof remains a shared, occupied space.

Engineering and execution must protect residents, workers, the building, and future maintenance access. Final requirements depend on site conditions and applicable standards.

Structural suitability

Confirm loading, anchoring approach, wind exposure, corrosion protection, and any engineer review required.

Roof and drainage protection

Protect waterproofing, drainage paths, parapets, services, and access while deciding structure placement.

Electrical protection

Document earthing, surge and isolation protection, cable management, labeling, shutdown, and fire-safety interfaces.

Safe access

Keep maintenance paths, edge risks, ladders, roof entries, and restricted areas practical for long-term use.

Monitoring and records

Retain approved drawings, test reports, warranties, serial records, approvals, generation data, and service history.

Planned maintenance

Agree cleaning frequency, inspection scope, response expectations, and responsibilities before commissioning.

Society Solar FAQs

Questions committees should settle early

Does a housing society need AGM approval?

The exact requirement depends on the society’s governing documents and applicable law, but a formal committee or general-body approval is commonly needed before committing shared roof space and funds. Obtain professional legal or compliance advice where required.

Which electricity loads can society solar support?

It is commonly evaluated against shared loads such as lifts, pumps, lighting, security, clubhouses, and facility equipment. The final arrangement depends on meters, sanctioned load, operating hours, and local DISCOM rules.

Is subsidy guaranteed for our society?

No. Eligibility, capacity, equipment, vendor, application, inspection, and commissioning conditions are governed by the current scheme and DISCOM process. Assistance should be treated as subject to approval, not as a guaranteed discount.

How much roof space will we need?

That depends on module dimensions, capacity, shade, row spacing, structure, access paths, tanks, services, and safety setbacks. A measured site survey is necessary before confirming usable area.

Can solar provide backup for lifts during an outage?

A standard on-grid system normally shuts down during a grid outage for safety. Backup requires a properly designed hybrid or storage solution, critical-load planning, suitable controls, and a separate commercial evaluation.

What should we compare between quotations?

Compare usable capacity, generation assumptions, module and inverter details, structure, electrical protections, approvals, taxes, exclusions, warranties, monitoring, maintenance, timeline, payment terms, and accountability.
