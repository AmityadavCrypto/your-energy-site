Business Use Cases

Solar planning shaped by how the property operates.

Different facilities have different load curves, roof constraints, safety requirements, and decision criteria. The project scope should reflect those differences.

01

Factories and manufacturing

Match generation with production shifts, sanctioned load, maximum demand, sensitive equipment, and shutdown windows.

02

Warehouses and logistics

Evaluate large metal roofs, skylights, ventilation, roof warranty, structural capacity, fire paths, and future expansion.

03

Offices and campuses

Align solar with weekday daytime demand, HVAC loads, multiple meters, parking areas, and facility-management processes.

04

Retail and hospitality

Account for long operating hours, refrigeration or cooling, customer access, roof services, appearance, and backup expectations.

05

Schools and institutions

Review seasonal occupancy, holidays, safety controls, procurement requirements, budget cycles, and future electricity demand.

06

Multi-site businesses

Prioritize sites using a consistent scorecard for tariff, consumption, roof quality, ownership, approvals, and expected returns.

Investment Case

Build the proposal from measurable assumptions.

A strong commercial solar decision is not based on a headline savings percentage. It connects the electrical data, engineering scope, contract terms, and operational risks to a transparent financial model.

A

Consumption and tariff baseline

Use recent bills and, where available, interval data to understand energy charges, demand, operating hours, and seasonal variation.

B

Generation model

State capacity, irradiation source, shading, orientation, losses, degradation, export assumptions, and expected annual output.

C

Complete project cost

Include modules, inverters, structure, electrical work, approvals, monitoring, civil work, taxes, exclusions, and operating costs.

D

Return and sensitivity

Compare payback or cash flow under realistic changes in tariff, generation, downtime, financing cost, and maintenance expense.

Commercial Models

Choose the structure that fits capital, control, and risk.

Model availability and terms vary by location, counterparty, credit assessment, regulation, and project scale. Obtain financial, tax, and legal advice before signing.

01

CAPEX ownership

The business funds and owns the system, receives the energy benefit, and remains responsible for asset management and agreed maintenance.

02

Financed ownership

A loan or lease may spread payments while preserving an ownership path, subject to credit, security, documentation, and lender terms.

03

OPEX / RESCO

A third party may invest and supply solar electricity under contract. Review tariff, tenure, escalation, minimum purchase, roof rights, and exit clauses.

i
Approvals are location and project specific.

Net metering, captive use, connectivity, equipment eligibility, and application processes depend on current central, state, DISCOM, and electrical rules. Commercial projects should not assume residential subsidy eligibility.

Technical Scope

Details that protect output, safety, and operations.

The final design should be prepared and reviewed by qualified professionals for the actual site, equipment, applicable standards, and utility requirements.

Roof-specific structure

Design for RCC or metal roofing, loading, wind, corrosion, fastening, waterproofing, roof warranty, and maintenance access.

Electrical integration

Check LT or HT architecture, transformer loading, panel capacity, protection coordination, isolation, earthing, and shutdown needs.

Fire and access planning

Preserve walkways, exits, equipment clearances, firefighting access, labels, emergency isolation, and safe work zones.

Equipment and warranty fit

Compare specifications, service access, warranty terms, approved models where applicable, compatibility, and lifecycle support.

Quality documentation

Retain drawings, calculations, inspection records, serial numbers, test reports, approvals, manuals, and handover documents.

Performance management

Define monitoring access, reporting, expected response, cleaning, preventive maintenance, spares, and escalation responsibility.

Commercial Solar FAQs

Questions to answer before approving the project

How is a commercial solar system sized?

Sizing should consider interval or monthly consumption, daytime load, tariff, sanctioned load, roof area, shading, export rules, operating schedule, and future demand. Roof area alone is not enough.

Can solar power the business during a grid outage?

A standard on-grid system normally shuts down when the grid fails. Backup requires a deliberately designed hybrid, battery, or compatible microgrid arrangement and a separate review of critical loads and controls.

Can solar be installed on a metal roof?

Often yes, but the roof profile, age, structural capacity, corrosion, fastening method, waterproofing, warranty, skylights, and safe access must be assessed before design.

Does commercial solar receive the residential rooftop subsidy?

Commercial and industrial projects should not assume residential central financial assistance. Any incentive, tax treatment, or state benefit must be checked against current rules with qualified advisers.

What is the difference between CAPEX and OPEX?

Under CAPEX the business generally funds and owns the asset. Under an OPEX or RESCO arrangement, a third party may own the system and sell energy under a long-term contract. Costs, savings, control, risk, and exit terms differ.

What should a commercial quotation include?

It should clearly state capacity, energy assumptions, equipment, structure, protections, approvals, civil and electrical scope, exclusions, taxes, schedule, warranties, monitoring, maintenance, payment terms, and performance responsibilities.
